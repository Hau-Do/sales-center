Keyloop Technical Assessment · Scenario C

System Design Document - Sales Center

A sales lead management tool for a UK dealership group: one place to see every incoming enquiry, work it, and prove it was worked. This document states the business case, the architecture, the decisions that were genuinely contested, the judgment calls about where the design would break, and how the whole thing gets built and released by one person.

PART 1

Business Context

What this solves for the user, what it is worth to the business.

1.1 The problem being solved

A dealership's sales floor does not lose deals because nobody can sell. It loses them in the gap between an enquiry arriving and a human touching it. Keyloop's own published research puts numbers on that gap:

Published findings that shaped what this tool prioritises.
Finding What it means on the sales floor What the product therefore does
20–30% of enquiries are lost simply because they were never logged or never followed up (OC&C 2024, cited by Keyloop) The inbox opens as a triage surface, sorted by what is about to breach, and states out loud how many enquiries have never been touched
69% of dealers cite rekeying and double entry across systems (OC&C 2024, cited by Keyloop) One lead record, one timeline. Every derived number — the SLA state, the working minutes, the triage counts — is computed, never retyped
8–10 systems used to close a single deal, costing 5–10 hours per person per week (keyloop.com/sales-hub) The enquiry, its whole activity history and its pipeline stage live on one record, not three

For the sales executive

They open one screen and it tells them what to do next. The response clock counts working minutes against the showroom's actual opening hours — including Sunday trading and bank holidays — so an enquiry that arrives at 19:40 on a Saturday is not reported as catastrophically late at 09:00 on Monday. Logging a call takes one dialog and persists immediately; the SLA clock stops server-side, from the activity itself, not from a checkbox someone has to remember to tick.

For the sales manager

They get a pipeline board showing where every open deal actually is, and stage guards that refuse a move the process does not allow — so the board reflects the floor rather than a parallel spreadsheet. Reporting is deliberately not in this phase: the activity log is the primary record, and analytics built over a log people have not yet got into the habit of filling teaches managers to distrust the tool. Once the log is reliably full, the numbers can be derived from it without a reconciliation step.

For the business

Value Mechanism How it would be measured in production
Revenue recovered from unworked leads Unworked count is the first thing on screen; SLA chips escalate before breach Count of leads with zero customer contact after 24 working hours → target zero
Faster speed-to-lead Working-minutes SLA per source; at-risk threshold at 25% of budget remaining p50 / p90 minutes to first genuine contact, by source and by executive
Less admin, fewer errors One record and one timeline; every derived figure computed, never rekeyed Hours per executive per week on data entry; correction rate on logged activity
Auditable compliance Immutable chronological activity log; marketing consent with lawful basis per channel FCA / GDPR audit sampling passes without manual reconstruction

Each is demonstrable by a command or a click.

  • Lead Inbox Triage bar, saved views, search, two sort orders, SLA chips
  • Lead Details + chronological activity log Day-grouped timeline, anomaly surfacing, SLA explainer

  • E-signature and quote sharing
  • Predictive ML analytics
PART 2

High-Level Architecture

Five views: what the system talks to, how one user action moves through it, how the frontend is decomposed, what the data looks like, and the contract between the two sides.

2.1 System Context Diagram

The Sales Center is a browser single-page application. In this phase it is self-contained: every external system it will eventually depend on is intercepted inside the browser by an MSW v2 service worker, which is the single integration boundary. Nothing in the application code knows it is talking to a mock — the UI issues real fetch calls to real /api/v1/… URLs.

What each external system supplies, and what happens without it

External system What we need from it Behaviour today · degradation if it is down
DMS (Keyloop Fusion) Vehicle stock, customer master record, order write-back Seeded stock in the mock. Degrades to read-only: quote and save locally, queue write-back
OEM portals / marketplaces Inbound enquiries with source attribution Seed generator produces them. If a feed stops, existing leads are unaffected; intake stalls and must alarm
DVLA licence check Confirmation a licence is valid before a test drive Recorded as a dated activity with a 365-day validity window. Fallback is a manual check logged by hand
Messaging gateway Sending email / SMS / WhatsApp to the customer Contact is logged, not sent. Fallback is an outbox: record the intent, retry the send

2.2 Data flow

The sequence diagram below is Part A's third requirement (Activity Logging) because it is the path that touches every layer. The detail worth noticing is that the SLA clock is stopped server-side: the handler asks the domain whether this activity counts as genuine customer contact. The UI cannot talk its way out of a missed target, and the same answer is given whether the question comes from the screen or the API.

2.3 Frontend component design

The architecture is a Layered (hexagonal) shape adapted to a browser app. One rule holds it together:

The organising constraint

Every dealership rule lives in src/domain/ as pure TypeScript — no React, no fetch, no DOM, and no ambient clock or randomness. React is a thin renderer of domain output. Time, ids and randomness enter only through injected ports (Clock, IdGenerator, Rng).

This is not a stylistic preference. It is what makes correctness demonstrable: the SLA engine, the working-hours calendar and the stage guards are all plain functions over plain data, unit-tested at 100% coverage — and the same functions run inside the mock API, so the rules cannot drift between what the screen believes and what the server enforces.

Responsibilities, module by module

Module Responsibility Why it is a separate layer
src/domain/ Every dealership rule, as pure functions over plain data Testable without a browser; reused verbatim by the mock server
src/ports/ Clock, IdGenerator, Rng A single, auditable channel for nondeterminism
src/data/ The Db seam and two implementations Makes “storage is swappable” a passing contract suite, not a claim
src/mocks/ MSW worker, handlers, deterministic seed generator An executable specification of the API we need from the backend team
src/api/ Real HTTP client, real URLs, correlation ids, typed errors The only module that knows HTTP exists — a real backend is a base-URL change
src/app/ Providers, routing, shell, and the one ticking clock A single interval drives every countdown on screen
src/features/ Screen composition only — no business logic Thin by construction; the logic is always one layer below
src/components/ Radix-based primitives and shared display pieces Accessibility solved once, not per screen
src/observability/ Structured logger, correlation, Web Vitals, error boundary, panel Observability a reviewer can see without opening devtools

State ownership

Two owners, no overlap. TanStack Query owns all server state (leads, activities, reference data) — no server data is ever copied into another store. The URL owns shareable state (?view=, ?sort=, and the __-prefixed bootstrap parameters). What is left — a sort toggle, the theme, whether the telemetry panel is open — is local component state, because a store for three booleans is ceremony rather than architecture. The payoff beyond tidiness is that Query's useIsFetching() and useIsMutating() give the end-to-end suite a real quiescence signal — which is why there is no cy.wait() anywhere in the Cypress suite.

2.4 Data model (Optional)

The model is deliberately small and lead-centric: one aggregate root (the lead), one append-only child collection (the activity log), two reference collections (sites and sales executives), and one singleton holding the scenario clock. Everything else — the customer, the vehicle, the part-exchange, the consent records — is an embedded value object living inside the lead document rather than a table of its own.

That shape follows from what the tool is. A dealership enquiry is a conversation about one person and one car, and the systems of record for customers and stock belong to the DMS. Embedding them keeps the enquiry self-contained and, more usefully, makes the vehicle of interest a snapshot as recorded rather than a pointer into live stock — so a later change in the stock system cannot silently rewrite what was discussed last week.

2.5 APIs and interfaces (Optional)

The mock backend exposes eight REST endpoints under /api/v1. They are not a convenience layer for the UI — they are the contract the real backend has to honour, written as executable code so the backend team can test against it rather than interpret prose. The application issues ordinary fetch calls to these paths; only the base URL would change.

Endpoint What it does · domain function Success Failure
GET /reference Sites, executives and the scenario meta — one call at start-up 200 {sites, executives, meta} 503
GET /leads The inbox. Filters on status, siteId and assignedTo 200 {leads, total} 503
GET /leads/:leadId The lead record and its whole timeline in one payload 200 {lead, activities} 404, 503
GET /leads/:leadId/activities The timeline alone 200 {activities} 404, 503
POST /leads/:leadId/activities Logs an activity and may stop the SLA clock, decided by isCustomerContact() 201 {activity, lead} 404, 422 INVALID_ACTIVITY_TYPE, 503
PATCH /leads/:leadId/stage Moves the lead — calls the same moveStage() the optimistic UI called 200 {lead} 404, 422 UNKNOWN_STAGE or any guard, 503
POST /leads/:leadId/close Won or lost — markWon() / markLost() 200 {lead} 404, 422 LOST_REASON_REQUIRED, HANDOVER_REQUIRED, LEAD_CLOSED, 503
POST /leads/:leadId/reopen reopenLead() 200 {lead} 404, 422 LEAD_ALREADY_OPEN, 503

Envelope conventions

Every success response is a named-key object, never a bare array, so a field can be added without breaking a client. Every failure is the same envelope — { error: { code, message, remedy, details? } } — and the remedy is not decoration: the UI renders it verbatim as the label on a one-click fix, and the identical string is asserted by both a unit test and an E2E test. Only 200, 201, 404, 422 and 503 are ever returned.

Error codes, and where each one is minted

The split below is the API-level evidence for the architecture: the handler validates the request, and every judgement about the business is passed through verbatim from the pure domain layer.

Family Minted in Codes
Lookup and transport handler NOT_FOUND (404) · UPSTREAM_UNAVAILABLE (503, injected)
Request validation handler INVALID_ACTIVITY_TYPE · UNKNOWN_STAGE · LOST_REASON_REQUIRED
Pipeline rules domain, passed through LEAD_CLOSED · SAME_STAGE · BACKWARD_LIMIT_EXCEEDED · HANDOVER_REQUIRED · LEAD_ALREADY_OPEN
Stage guards domain, passed through LICENCE_CHECK_REQUIRED · DEPOSIT_REQUIRED · PREPARATION_REQUIRED_BEFORE_HANDOVER — each carries the remedy the UI turns into a button
Client-side only api/client.ts NETWORK_ERROR — synthesised when fetch itself rejects, so a dead network reaches the UI in the same shape as a refusal

A trade-off is only real if the cost is named. Each row below states what was given up.

The decisions that had a credible alternative.

React + Vite Next.js An internal tool behind a login has no SEO requirement and no anonymous traffic. PART 4

System Design Judgment

Where this breaks, what it depends on, and what it does when something it depends on goes away.

4.1 Bottlenecks — what fails first under a spike

Today every request is served inside the browser, so there is no server to overload. The load that matters is therefore data volume per user: a group with fifteen sites and a busy plate-change month. The table below names the real limits in the code as written, in the order they would bite.

Pressure point Risk What actually breaks Mitigation
localStorage as the store First to break Synchronous, main-thread, ~5 MB per origin, and it serialises the whole collection on every write. At a few thousand leads the write cost becomes a visible jank on every activity logged; past the quota it throws It is already a seam. localStorageDb degrades to memory on failure today; the production path is IndexedDB or, properly, a real API — one class, no caller changes
GET /leads returns everything First to break No pagination: every matching lead comes back in one payload, and total merely echoes the array length rather than describing a page window. The client then sorts and filters the whole set on the main thread Cursor pagination with the sort applied server-side, so the payload is bounded by the page rather than by the dealership. Removing propensity scoring already took an O(leads × activities) loop out of this handler
One-second clock tick × every SLA chip Large inbox ClockProvider ticks at 1 s and every countdown re-renders. With an unvirtualised list of a thousand rows that is a thousand re-renders per second Virtualise the list; drop the tick to 30 s for targets more than an hour out. The clock is already a single injected port, so this is one change in one place
Working-minutes calculation Bounded The business-hours walk iterates day by day, capped at 400 iterations. Cheap per call, but it runs per lead per tick Memoise per (receivedAt, source) pair; the function is pure, so caching is safe by construction
Service worker boot Dev only Every request passes through MSW. It is a fixed per-request cost that disappears entirely in production None needed — the mock is deleted when a real API lands

4.2 Dependency risk

This phase was deliberately built so that no other team can block it. That is the purpose of mocking at the network boundary rather than waiting for an API. The risks that remain are about the handover, not the build.

Dependency Risk Exposure How it is de-risked
Backend / data team — the real lead and activity API Medium The largest one. If their contract differs from ours, integration is rework rather than a swap The MSW handlers are the contract — 8 endpoints with request and response shapes, error codes and status mapping, all executable. The backend team gets a specification they can test against instead of a conversation. Blast radius of a mismatch is one adapter module, src/api/client.ts
Identity / SSO team Low No authentication in this phase; the client holds no token logic at all Every request already goes through one client module, so auth arrives as a request interceptor plus a 401 handler. Nothing else changes
DMS team (Keyloop Fusion) Medium Stock, customer master and order write-back. Write-back is the genuinely hard part — it is someone else's system of record Reads are cacheable and degrade to stale. Writes should be queued and idempotent from the start; the activity log already gives an audit trail to reconcile against
DVLA and the messaging gateway Low Each is a third party with its own availability Each has a manual fallback that is already the current process: log the licence check by hand, record the contact as an activity
Library vendors Low React, Radix, TanStack Query, Tailwind, MSW — all MIT and widely adopted. MSW is dev-only, so it carries no production risk The one with real lock-in is TanStack Query, because it shapes the data layer. Accepted deliberately: it is the most stable piece of the set
Node 25 on the build machine Resolved An odd-numbered, non-LTS runtime that several current majors exclude Versions pinned to the widest-compatible line; CI runs a Node 22 / 24 / 25 matrix so a regression on any of them fails the build

4.3 Fallback plan

The question is whether a failure surfaces an error or recovers. The honest answer differs by failure, and differs between this phase and production — so both are stated.

Failure Behaviour today Production behaviour
Read request fails (5xx / network) A typed ApiError surfaces an error panel carrying the failure's remedy string and its correlation id. No retry — deliberate, so the fault-injection demo is honest Retry idempotent GETs three times with exponential backoff and jitter, then fall back to stale-while-error: render the last good cached data behind a “showing data from 09:14” banner. A stale inbox beats an empty one
Write fails (logging an activity) The mutation surfaces the error and the dialog stays open with the user's text intact — the one thing that must never be lost is what the executive just typed Attach an idempotency key per activity, queue the write in an outbox, retry in the background, and reconcile on reconnect. Never silently drop
Business rule rejects the action Not a failure — a guard. The API returns 422 with {code, message, remedy}, and the UI renders the remedy as a one-click fix (“Log licence check”). The same string is asserted by both the unit test and the E2E test Unchanged. This is the designed behaviour
localStorage unavailable or over quota Already handled. localStorageDb degrades to an in-memory store, warns once, and the application keeps working — private browsing and full-disk are both covered Same pattern against IndexedDB, with the outbox above so nothing is lost when storage is the thing that failed
Third party down (lender, valuation, DVLA, messaging) Not called in this phase Degrade, never block. Quote locally and queue the proposal (“submitted — awaiting lender”); fall back to manual entry for valuations; record the contact intent in an outbox when the gateway is unreachable. Circuit-break per partner so one slow integration cannot exhaust the connection pool
A component throws while rendering An error boundary catches it, logs ui.render_error with the component stack, and shows a recoverable panel instead of a white screen Same, with the log line shipped to the collector — the correlation id already links it to the request that preceded it
Service worker fails to register Known gap. main.tsx awaits worker.start() before rendering and does not catch a rejection, so a failure to register the worker would leave a blank page rather than an explanation Moot in production — there is no worker. For the demo build the fix is small and worth making: catch the rejection and render a static “mock backend failed to start” panel

Observability

Layer Implementation Where you see it
Correlation ids Minted per request, echoed by every handler On the request and response log lines
Structured logging {level, msg, at, correlationId, ...}; warn/error as one-line JSON docs/observability.md

Ids are sequential, not crypto.randomUUID(), so a Cypress run is reproducible and a spec can assert on a specific trace.

In production this is a transport swap: logger's emit posts to an OTLP collector or Sentry, and the correlation id becomes the trace id that joins the browser span to the backend span.

PART 6

How GenAI was used in the design phase

The implementation narrative is in the README. This section covers design, where GenAI was used differently - and where its output required the most scrutiny.

The judges were instructed to recompute, not merely to opine

That produced concrete findings:

  • They recomputed every worked finance example and found arithmetic or formula errors in all three proposals. One quoted a PCP payment of £328.14 where its own formula produced £367.75. This work informed verification discipline even though the finance enhancement was later removed from the MVP.
  • One judge identified that a proposed E2E strategy depended on cy.request reaching MSW, which it cannot do because Cypress sends that request from its Node process. That finding became §3.2 and changed the application's startup sequence.
  • A proposed frozen demonstration clock landed on a Sunday morning outside the configured opening hours, which would have flattened the SLA countdowns the demo needed to show.

Confident library claims were verified by execution

Two research findings proved false when the code ran: eslint-plugin-react-hooks@7.1.1's recommended-latest configuration still used the legacy ESLint shape, and jsdom@30 excluded Node 25. A third assumption - that rendered oklch() contrast could be predicted reliably with a naive sRGB conversion - failed near the gamut edge. In that case, cypress-axe became the arbiter instead of the generated analysis.

The resulting division of labour was deliberate

GenAI was effective at breadth: enumerating UK motor-retail vocabulary, drafting realistic seed data and generating exhaustive test cases. It was less reliable for arithmetic and current library behaviour. The working rule therefore became: let GenAI propose, then require an independent mechanism to adjudicate. The mutation sanity check, accessibility gate, deterministic E2E contract and 144-pair pipeline table are retained examples of that rule. The independent IRR oracle was another example and was retired with the finance engine when the scope was reduced to the MVP.

PART 7

Rollout

Turning the design above into backlog items: how the work reaches users, who does it, and in what order it ships.

7.1 Rollout Strategy

This phase ships no production backend, so what follows is the plan for when the real API lands. It is recorded now rather than later because the shape of a rollout is a design decision — the cohort you expose first determines what the pilot can actually tell you.

Staged rollout. Each stage has an explicit promotion test rather than a judgement call.
Stage Cohort Promote when — measured from the activity log the tool already keeps
0 · Dogfood Me plus one sales manager, on seeded data All seven E2E journeys pass on real showroom hardware and a tablet
1 · Pilot site One dealership, all of its executives (~7% of users) Speed-to-lead p90 no worse than the incumbent; zero leads unworked past 24 working hours; no P1 defect; activity-logging rate above the old system's
2 · One brand Every site of a single franchise brand Stage 1 metrics hold at roughly 5× the volume; support contacts per user flat or falling
3 · Group All sites Stage 2 metrics hold across every brand in the group
